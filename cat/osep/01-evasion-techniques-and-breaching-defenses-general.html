PEN-300 Module 1 — Evasion Techniques and Breaching Defenses: General Course Information

Fuente: material del curso correspondiente (sin video público)

Objective

Course-introduction module: terminology, provided material, lab mechanics, restrictions, and the OSEP exam format. No attacks, no exercises, no course videos (this is one of the modules explicitly excluded from video coverage, along with Operating System and Programming Theory and Trying Harder: The Labs).

Positioning of the course

  • Audience: security professionals with existing offensive experience; PEN-200 (OSCP) or equivalent knowledge is the stated prerequisite.
  • Goal: offensive techniques that work against client organizations with hardened systems — client-side code execution, antivirus evasion, application whitelisting bypasses, network detection bypasses; second half covers lateral movement, pivoting, and advanced Active Directory attacks.
  • Scope line the course draws explicitly: PEN-300 is an advanced penetration testing course, not a Red Team course. It generally does not deal with evading a blue team; it focuses on bypassing automated security mechanisms that block an attack.
  • Engagement taxonomy defined up front:
  • Assumed breach test — tester is given standard/low-privileged access to an internal system; focus is the internal network.
  • External test — social engineering plus attacks against internet-facing infrastructure.
  • Red team / adversary simulation — goal is testing the client's security personnel (blue team) by mimicking advanced attacker TTPs; techniques overlap with pentesting but the goals differ.

Provided material

Course book modules + complementary course videos (read/watch both — depth alternates between the two per module); per-chapter exercises (some with optional, harder extra mile exercises); VPN lab access; student forum; live support (Discord); one OSEP exam attempt. Subscription-based access renews automatically unless cancelled.

Practical note carried into later modules: copy-pasting code from the book into scripts may introduce unintended whitespace/newlines from formatting — retype or clean listings before use.

Lab mechanics

  • Control panel: revert client/lab machines per module; 12 reverts per 24 h (reset 00:00 GMT+0), minimum 5 minutes between reverts of the same machine set; a revert always reverts all VMs of the selected module (no per-VM revert) — late-course AD environments take a while. Extra reverts via Student Administrators (help@offensive-security.com).
  • VMs are dedicated per student, not shared. Client machines from only one module can be active at a time — selecting a new module removes the previous module's machines.
  • Machines: Windows 10, Windows Server 2019, Ubuntu 20.04.
  • Long VPN disconnection removes active VMs; on reconnect a revert is required — copy notes/scripts off to the Kali VM before disconnecting.
  • Post-module challenges simulate black-box engagements: no credentials provided.
  • Kali Linux is the supported platform (commands shown as non-root user on Kali; support is for Kali on VMware).

Lab restrictions (strictly enforced; violations can disable lab access)

  • No ARP spoofing or any other poisoning / man-in-the-middle attacks against the network.
  • No brute force attacks against the VPN infrastructure.
  • No attempts to hack other students' clients or Kali machines.

OSEP exam

  • Single large private network to attack and compromise. Pass by obtaining access to a specific section of the network or at least 100 points from individual machines.
  • 47 h 45 min lab time, fully dedicated environment; remotely proctored (identity verification 15 min before start, VPN pack issued then).
  • 24 h afterward for the report; graded on report quality/accuracy and reproducibility of findings. Results within ten business days.
  • Booking via the exam scheduling calendar (labs can fill — schedule early); exam guide is the authoritative reference.
